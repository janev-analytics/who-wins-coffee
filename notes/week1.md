## Dutch Bros Item 1 (FY2025, year ended Dec 31, 2025)
Source: [10-K](paste link)

**1. Shop growth**
- 2025 openings: 141 company-operated + 13 franchised = 154, no closures. 982 → 1,136 shops = +15.7%.
- Management's target is "mid-teens" annual shop growth; 15.7% means the goal was achieved in 2025 (one year, not yet a trend).
- Franchised openings fell 43% (23 → 13) while company-operated rose (128 → 141). Consistent with the strategy since 2017: mostly company-operated; franchisees only infill their high-demand markets.

**2. Typical shop**
- Formats: ~500 sq ft (legacy), ~800–1,000 (current), ~1,000–1,200 (endcap). Lots of 25,000+ sq ft.
- ~85% of sales through drive-thru, 15% walk-up, no seating.
- vs Starbucks: smaller shops without seating mean lower rent and build cost per shop. Starbucks ("Back to Starbucks") is investing in the café experience instead.

**3. AUV and same shop sales** (USD thousands)
- AUV = average annual sales per shop. Systemwide $2,115K (~$2.1M) vs company-operated $2,061K.
- Same shop sales growth: 2.8% (2023) → 5.3% (2024) → 5.6% (2025).
- Ticket = average spend per order; transactions = number of orders. Sales growth ≈ ticket + transactions.
- 2023: ticket +7.3%, transactions −4.5%. 2025: ticket +2.4%, transactions +3.2%.

**Hypothesis: why franchised shops have higher AUV**
- Franchised shops are older and in established West Coast markets; many company-operated shops are new, in states where the brand is still unknown.
- Based on: "since 2017 company-operated strategy", "franchisees infill high-demand markets", founded in Oregon (1992).
- Test 1 (age), confirmed: since the start of 2023, company-operated shops 396 → 811 (~51% are under 3 years old); franchised 275 → 325 (~15%).
- Test 2 (location): check Item 2 Properties, shops by state, company vs franchise. Still open.

**4. Franchise economics**
- Royalty % not disclosed (Note 3: "a percentage of net franchise sales").
- Take rate = franchising revenue / franchise sales = 122,046 / (2,223,576 − 1,509,329) = **17.1%**.
- Franchisees report their sales to Dutch Bros, which applies its % to calculate royalties and marketing fees. Systemwide sales are unaudited, so 17.1% is an estimate.
- In plain words: for every $1 a franchised shop sells, Dutch Bros earns ~17 cents (royalty + marketing fee + bean and product sales) without investing in the shop.
- Two measures, don't mix: franchise segment contribution 76.9% (before D&A) vs franchise gross profit margin 72.6%.
