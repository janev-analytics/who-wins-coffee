# Week 0 Notes

## Terms I learned
1. **Partner (Starbucks):** Starbucks' word for an employee. Employees get stock (Bean Stock), so they're called partners. Not the same as a business partner (licensees, Nestlé, JVs).
2. **Stock-based compensation (SBC):** A real expense (Damodaran, Buffett). Recorded on the income statement, no cash out, but it dilutes existing shareholders. Buybacks used to offset dilution DO cost cash. Rule: never add SBC back when judging profit or free cash flow, and don't count it twice.
3. **52/53-week fiscal year:** Starbucks' FY ends on the Sunday closest to Sep 30. Years are 364 days; every ~5–6 years a 53rd week is added (FY2021, next FY2027). See sources.md.
4. **Franchise vs licensed store:** Franchise = brand's name, independent owner pays rent/staff and pays the brand fees, royalties and buys its products (Tim Hortons, 325 Dutch Bros shops). Licensed store = brand's store inside another business (Starbucks in Target, airports). Starbucks doesn't franchise in the U.S.
5. **Segment contribution (Dutch Bros):** Segment revenue minus direct segment costs, before depreciation, SG&A and interest.

## Business models: the four ways coffee brands make money

|---|---|---|---|
| Company-operated | The brand | The brand | 811 Dutch Bros shops; most Starbucks in North America |
| Franchise | The brand | Independent owner | Almost all Tim Hortons; 325 Dutch Bros shops |

| Wholesale / consumer goods | Product brand in someone else's shop | Retailer | Starbucks bags in grocery (Channel Development, Nestlé) |

## Dutch Bros FY2025 (USD thousands)
- 1,136 shops at Dec 31, 2025: 811 company-operated (71%), 325 franchised (29%), 25 states.
- Company-operated margin: **28.9%** (436,605 / 1,509,329)
- Franchise margin: **76.9%** (99,094 / 128,830)
- Revenue growth: **+27.9%** (1,638,159 vs 1,281,015)
- **Why the gap:** the franchisee pays rent, labor and food. Dutch Bros only collects fees, royalties and bean sales, so few costs sit against that revenue. The gap is even bigger after depreciation, because company shops are built with Dutch Bros' own capital.
- **Lesson:** never compare margins across models. Compare profit per dollar invested (ROIC).

**How licensed stores make money**
- Licensed stores = 12% of total revenue.
- Starbucks earns a margin on products sold to the licensee + a royalty on retail sales. The licensee pays operating costs and capital.
- Starbucks' own words: licensed stores have "lower gross margin and higher operating margin" than company-operated. Same lesson as Dutch Bros.
- ⚠️ In some international markets Starbucks uses real franchising but counts it as "licensed".

**Coffee supply**

## Concepts: SBC and free cash flow

**Why SBC is added back in the cash flow statement**
- Employees were paid in shares, not cash. The expense lowered profit, but no cash left the company, so GAAP adds it back to get operating cash flow.
- The cash is real (it stayed in the company), but it isn't free: shareholders paid through dilution.
- If the company buys back shares to offset dilution, that cash leaves through Financing, which FCF ignores. The cost "escapes" the metric.

**My rule (same for every company)**
- How much does the business earn? → FCF = operating cash flow − SBC − capex. Always subtract SBC.
- What does management do with the cash? → buybacks, dividends, debt paydown. Never subtract buybacks from FCF (that's distribution, not a cost).
- Never subtract both SBC and buybacks (double counting).

**FCF vs liquidity**
- FCF = cash the business generates in a year after costs and reinvestment (a flow). It's what owners can take out, and what a company's value is based on (Damodaran: value = present value of future FCF).
- Liquidity = can the company pay bills due soon (a stock: cash on hand, current ratio).
- Profit isn't cash (Foundations S3).
- For my series: FCF ÷ invested capital = which model generates more free cash per dollar invested.

**Starbucks cash flow FY2025, things to remember**
- Net earnings fell almost by half: $3,762M → $1,857M. Loss on disposal/impairment jumped to $834.7M (from $121.5M), which is the restructuring (Note 18).
- Leases are huge: non-cash lease costs $1,513.8M; operating lease liability payments ($1,576.7M). "Leases are debt": this is where company-operated and franchise models differ most.
